The proposed wording was reviewed against the existing agreement to ensure that the revised process does not unintentionally alter the responsibilities already accepted by the parties. The draft keeps the common baseline but permits limited exceptions where the circumstances are documented and the appropriate approval has been obtained.

The parties discussed the sequence to be followed when an agreed step cannot be completed as originally planned. The revised text should distinguish mandatory obligations from actions that depend on local judgement. It should also identify the evidence that must be retained when an exception is accepted and clarify who has authority to approve it.

A previous decision was considered during the review but was not treated as an automatic precedent. The surrounding circumstances were different in several respects, so the reasoning supporting the earlier position was recorded separately from the conclusion reached in the present case. This distinction is intended to reduce the risk that a future reader interprets the earlier decision as creating a broader obligation.

One section required revision because the same term could otherwise be understood differently in the operational schedule and in the main agreement. The parties agreed that the definition should remain aligned across both documents. No new commercial commitment was introduced by the proposed wording.

The practical sequence may change if information from the other party is not received before the next checkpoint. In that event, the affected step should remain open rather than being treated as completed solely because an internal action has been recorded. The parties also considered what notification, remediation or additional approval would be required if the agreed sequence cannot be followed.

The current draft is intended to clarify the process while preserving the overall objective and commercial position. Working assumptions remain explicit until the remaining dependency is resolved. Comments from the participating teams have been consolidated, and the next version will identify the accepted interpretation, the applicable exception process and the evidence required to support any departure from the agreed sequence.
